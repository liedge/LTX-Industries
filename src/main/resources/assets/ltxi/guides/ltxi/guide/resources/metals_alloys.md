# Metals and Alloys

## Titanium

<ItemImage id="titanium_ingot" scale="2" />

<ItemGrid>
<ItemIcon id="raw_titanium" />
<ItemIcon id="titanium_ore" />
<ItemIcon id="deepslate_titanium_ore" />
</ItemGrid>

Titanium is the foundation material for practically everything made by us. It generates in the Overworld in both the stone
and deepslate layers, with a bias towards the world floor. It is slightly rarer than iron.

## Silver

<ItemImage id="silver_ingot" scale="2" />

<ItemGrid>
<ItemIcon id="raw_silver" />
<ItemIcon id="silver_ore" />
<ItemIcon id="deepslate_silver_ore" />
</ItemGrid>

Silver is a precious metal used for mid-grade electronics and energy components. In most biomes, it generates deep underground
with a bias towards the world floor. It is slightly more common than gold. In mountains-type biomes, silver can generate
at higher altitudes and is much more common.

## Niobium

<ItemImage id="niobium_ingot" scale="2" />

<ItemGrid>
<ItemIcon id="raw_niobium" />
<ItemIcon id="niobium_ore" />
</ItemGrid>

Niobium is a rare precious metal used in high-end electronics, namely to fuse in dust form with [Silicon](elements_base.md#silicon)
and [Phosphorus](elements_base.md#phosphorus) in an [Argon](elements_base.md#argon) gas environment to form nanoprocessor
logic cores. It generates sparsely in the outer End Islands. The ore's distinctive purple traces glow, keep an eye
out on the terrain!

## Slatesteel

<ItemImage id="slatesteel_ingot" scale="2" />

<ItemGrid>
<ItemIcon id="minecraft:iron_ingot" />
<ItemIcon id="carbon_dust" />
<ItemIcon id="deepslate_dust" />
</ItemGrid>

Slatesteel is a basic alloy of iron, carbon, the minerals in deepslate dust, and [Oxygen](elements_base.md#oxygen) gas
to burn away the impurities. It lacks the versatility of titanium and is too heavy to be used as a chassis material,
but is perfect for high-stress applications.

## Tungsten-Plated Slatesteel

<ItemImage id="tungsten_slatesteel_ingot" scale="2" />

<ItemGrid>
<ItemIcon id="tungsten_trioxide" />
<ItemIcon id="slatesteel_dust" />
<ItemIcon id="tungsten_slatesteel_dust" />
</ItemGrid>

Even our machines struggle with isolating pure Tungsten metal. But what if it could be bound to a very sturdy metal
to produce an extraordinarily resilient alloy? And so, Tungsten-Plated Slatesteel was created.

Processing titanium ores through the [third stage](../ore_processing.md#stage-3) of the ore processing line may yield
Tungsten Trioxide as an occasional byproduct. Reducing the Tungsten Trioxide with [Hydrogen](elements_base.md#hydrogen)
in the presence of Slatesteel Dust will bind the Tungsten metal to the Slatesteel. The melting point of the resulting dust
is too high to be smelted in a regular furnace in air. It must be smelted in the [Arc Furnace](../machines/processing/arc_furnace.md)
and [Nitrogen](elements_base.md#nitrogen) gas must be used for shielding.

## Rhenium

<ItemImage id="rhenium_ingot" scale="2" />

<ItemGrid>
<ItemIcon id="rhenium_7_oxide" />
<ItemIcon id="ammonium_perrhenate" />
<ItemIcon id="rhenium_dust" />
</ItemGrid>

Rhenium metal is used to build our top of the line technology. Like Tungsten, it cannot be found in nature without
extensive recovery efforts. Being by collecting Rhenium(VII) Oxide from copper ores during the [fifth stage](../ore_processing.md#stage-5)
of the ore processing line. Reacting with [Ammonia](elements_compound.md#ammonia) in the [Chem Lab](../machines/processing/chem_lab.md)
will yield Ammonium Perrhenate, but this is still not in a usable state. Further reduction in the Chem Lab with
[Hydrogen](elements_base.md#hydrogen) gas will produce Rhenium dust. Finally, smelt the Rhenium dust in the Arc Furnace
to yield Rhenium Ingots. This process is lengthy and requires Argon gas for shielding.
